Dashboard for Twitch Transcripts

03 Nov 2026

TLDR

  • I transcribed HasanAbi's Twitch streams, from the archived video audio, with a speech recognition model.
  • I counted how much of each stream had speech and which words came up most.
  • I found that three Michigan politicians were named most often in the final streams before the midterms.
  • The panels below show hours, topics and words by stream. Methods are at the end.

People named

Names were found with spaCy, a language-processing library, which marks the words in a sentence that name a person. Mentions are grouped by surname, so "Stevens" alone counts as Haley Stevens. A first name alone, such as "Abdul", is not counted. The streamer himself, fictional characters and religious figures were left out. Each cell gives how often the person was named per 10,000 words in that stream.

Methods. The streams are the HasanAbi Twitch broadcasts transcribed so far, listed in the charts. New streams and older archived streams are added as they are transcribed. On days with many streams, each chart scrolls sideways. Dates are stream start dates in Pacific time. The audio of each archived stream was transcribed in full with WhisperX, using the Whisper large-v3 model. Speech hours are the summed length of the segments where the model wrote text. Words are counted as the model wrote them, split on spaces. The transcripts include every voice on stream: the host, guests, and audio from videos played on stream. Topic and word rates count word tokens, which are runs of letters with an optional apostrophe, per 10,000 tokens in the stream. A possessive counts as its word, so "Israel's" counts as "Israel". The speech recognition spells Abdul El-Sayed several ways, such as "Abdullah Al-Sayed" and "Abdel El-Sayed". All of them count as "Abdul El-Sayed". Each topic is a fixed word list, shown under its chart. The word grid opens on word pairs. A pair is two tokens spoken one after the other in the same segment. Neither token is a common English word, and each has at least three letters. Pairs with the streamer's own name were removed. The single-words view uses the 12 most frequent words over all eight streams after common English words were removed. Spoken filler that the common-word list misses was also removed, for example "thank" and "wait". So were first names whose surname is already counted, and the streamer's own names. Speech recognition makes mistakes, mostly in names and in crosstalk, so counts are approximate. This page shows only counts and rates; no transcript text is published.
