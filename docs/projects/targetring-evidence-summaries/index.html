Gathering Drug Target Evidence With a Model That Only Paraphrases

26 Oct 2026bioinformaticsllmtools

TLDR:

  • I set out to make a demo that aggregates the top genetic targets for a disease into one figure, to motivate biologists to take those genes into early in vitro target validation experiments.
  • I built a tool that turns a disease name into that figure: for each differentially expressed gene, its druggability, safety, tissue expression, drug pipeline, literature and mouse phenotypes.
  • I added a language model that writes one short evidence paragraph per gene from the gathered facts only, stored with the run, and never ranks, filters or decides anything.
  • The tool and the code are public.

targetring takes a disease, finds a public gene expression study for it, picks a comparison of affected against unaffected people, and draws a ring figure for the genes that differ. Each ring is one source: tractability and safety from Open Targets, tissue expression from GTEx, development level from Pharos, mechanisms from ChEMBL, literature counts from Europe PMC and PubTator, mouse phenotypes from MGI. A missing cell is drawn as missing, never as zero, because the figure used to be built by hand from six browser tabs and the hardest part was knowing whether an empty cell meant "not measured" or "the lookup failed". Clicking a gene opens a drawer with the full drug pipeline, the trials behind it, and a GO or NO-GO line from a fixed three part rule: expressed in a disease tissue, dysregulated in the study, and a phenotype on record.

The language model's job is deliberately small. For each gene the tool assembles the facts it already fetched, sends them once to a model with a prompt that forbids recommendations, rankings, invented citations and internal codes, and stores the paragraph with the run. The same facts always produce the same stored paragraph, so the four demo runs needed 13 calls for 46 genes, and the whole demo's model spend was $1.35, measured 18 October 2026 and shown on the tool's Paid calls tab. A second, smaller call reads the gene's mouse phenotype terms against the disease area and returns a 0 or 1, printed beside the rule-based phenotype check and never replacing it. The drawer below shows that line for MUC1 in the Crohn's run. The stored paragraph for the same gene begins with the verdict and its three reasons, then the fold change, the tissue expression against the median across 54 tissues, the mouse phenotypes, and the empty drug pipeline, each with its number and unit, and no opinion of its own.
